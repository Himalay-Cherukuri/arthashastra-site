Chapter XV: The Superintendent of Storehouse

B02.C15 · Book II: The Duties of Government Superintendents · pp. 129–137

The Superintendent of Storehouse

P001 THE superintendent of storehouse (Koshthágára) shall supervise the accounts of agricultural produce (síta); taxes coming under Ráshtra, country-parts; commerce (krayima); barter (parivartna); begging for grains (prámityaka); grains borrowed with promise to repay (ápamityaka); manufacture of rice, oils, etc. (simhanika); accidental revenue (anyajáta); statements to check expenditure (vyayapratyaya); and recovery of past arrears (upasthánam). p.130 anyajáta koshthágára prámityaka ráshtra síta upasthánam ápamityaka

P002 Whatever in the shape of agricultural produce is brought in by the superintendent of agriculture, (of crown-lands) is termed sítá. p.130 sítá

P003 The taxes that are fixed (pindakara), taxes that are paid in the form of one-sixth of produce (shadbhága), provision paid (by the people) for the army (senábhakta), taxes that are levied for religious purposes (bali), taxes or subsidies that are paid by vassal kings and others (kara), taxes that are specially collected on the occasion of the birth of a prince (utsanga), taxes that are collected when there is some margin left for such collection (pársva), compensation levied in the shape of grains for any damage done by cattle to crops (párihínaka), presentation made to the king, (aupáyanika), and taxes that are levied on lands below tanks, lakes, etc., built by the king (Kaushtheyaka),--all these come under the head „Ráshtra.‟ p.130 one king prince compensation aupáyanika párihínaka pársva ráshtra senábhakta shadbhága

P004 Sale proceeds of grains, grains purchased and the collection of interest in kind or grain debts (prayogapratyádána) are termed commerce. p.130 prayogapratyádána

P005 Profitable exchange of grains for grains is termed barter (parivarthana). p.130

P006 Grains collected by begging is termed prámityaka. p.130 prámityaka

P007 Grains borrowed with promise to repay the same is termed ápamityaka. p.131 ápamityaka

P008 Pounding (rice, etc.), dividing (pulses, etc.), frying (corns and beans), manufacture of beverages (suktakarma), manufacture of flour by employing those persons who live upon such works, extracting oil by employing shepherds and oil-makers, and manufacture of sugar from the juice of sugar-cane are termed simhanika. p.131

P009 Whatever is lost and forgotten (by others) and the like form accidental revenue (anyajáta). p.131 anyajáta

P010 Investment, the relic of a wrecked undertaking, and savings from an estimated outlay are the means to check expenditure (vyayapratyaya). p.131

P011 That amount or quantity of compensation which is claimed for making use of a different balance or for any error in taking a handful is termed vyáji. p.131 compensation vyáji

P012 Collection of arrears is termed „upasthána,‟ „recovery of past arrears.‟ p.131 upasthána

P013 Of grains, oils, sugar, and salt, all that concerns grains will be treated of in connection with the duties of the „Superintendent of Agriculture.‟ p.131 superintendent of agriculture

P014 Clarified butter, oil, serum of flesh, and pith or sap (of plants, etc.)., are termed oils (sneha). p.131

P015 Decoction (phánita), jaggory, granulated sugar, and sugar-candy are termed kshára. p.131 kshára phánita

P016 Saindhava, that which is the product of the country of Sindhu; Sámudra, that which is produced from seawater; Bida; Yavakshara, nitre, Sauvarchala, that which is the product of the country of suvarchala; and udbhedaja, that which is extracted from saline soil are termed lavana, salt. p.131 sámudra

P017 The honey of the bee as well as the juice extracted from grapes are called madhu. p.132

P018 Mixture made by combining any one of the substances, such as the juice of sugarcane, jaggory, honey,. the, juice of grapes, the essence of the fruits of jambu (Euginia Jambolana) and of jaka tree—with the essence of meshasringa (a kind of plant) and long pepper, with or without the addition of the essence of chirbhita (a kind of gourd), cucumber, sugar-cane, mango-fruit and the fruit of myrobalam, the mixture being prepared so as to last for a month, or six months, or a year, constitute the group of astringents (sukta-varga). p.132 one six

P019 The fruits of those trees which bear acid fruits, those of karamarda (Carissa Carandas), those of vidalámalka (myrobalam), those of matulanga (citron tree), those of kola (small jujuba), those of badara (Flacourtia Cataphracta), those of sauvíra (big jujuba), and those of parushaka (Grewia Asiatica) and the like come under the group of acid fruits. p.132 sauvíra vidalámalka

P020 Curds, acid prepared from grains and the like are acids in liquid form. p.132

P021 Long pepper, black pepper, ginger, cumin seed, kiratatikta (Agathotes Chirayta), white mustard, coriander, choraka (a plant), damanaka (Artemisia Indica), maruvaka (Vangueria Spinosa), sigru (Hyperanthera Moringa), and the like together with their roots (kánda) come under the group of pungent substances (tiktavarga). p.132 kánda

P022 Dried fish, bulbous roots (kándamúla), fruits and vegetables form the group of edibles (sakavarga). p.133 kándamúla

P023 Of the store, thus, collected, half shall be kept in reserve to ward off the calamities of the people and only the other half shall be used. Old collection shall be replaced by new supply. p.133 half

P024 The superintendent shall also personally supervise the increase or diminution sustained in grains when they are pounded (kshunna), or frayed (ghrishta), or reduced to flour (pishta), or fried (bhrashta), or dried after soaking in water. p.133

P025 The essential part (sára, i.e., that which is fit for food) of kodrava (Paspalam Scrobiculatum) and of vrihi (rice) is one-half; that of sáli (a kind of rice) is (half) less by one-eighth part; that of varaka (Phraseolus Trilobus) is (half) less by one-third part; that of priyangu (panic seed or millet) is one-half ; that of chamasi (barley), of mudga (Phraseolus Mungo) and of masha (Phraseolus Radiatus) is (half) less by one-eighth part; that of saibya (simbi) is one-half; that of masúra (Ervum Hirsutum) is (half) less by one-third part (than the raw material or grains from which it is prepared). p.133 half one masúra sáli sára masha

P026 Raw flour and kulmasha (boiled and forced rice) will be as much as one and a half of the original quantity of the grains. p.133 half one

P027 Barley gruel as well as its flour baked will be twice the original quantity. p.133 twice

P028 Kodrava (Paspalam Scrobiculatum), varaka (Phraseolus Trilobus), udáraka (Panicum), and priyangu (millet) will increase three times the original quantity when cooked. Vríhi (rice) will increase four times when cooked. Sáli (a kind of rice) will increase five times when cooked. p.133 five four three sáli udáraka vríhi

P029 Grains will increase twice the original quantity when moistened; and two and a half times when soaked to sprouting condition. p.134 half twice two

P030 Grains fried will increase by one-fifth the original quantity; leguminous seeds (kaláya), when fried, will increase twice the original; likewise rice when fried. p.134 one twice kaláya

P031 Oil extracted from atasi (linseed) will be one-sixth (of the quantity of the seed); that extracted from the seeds, nimba (Azadirachta Indica), kusámra (?), and Kapittha (Feronia Elephantum) will be one-fifth; and that extracted from tila (seasumum), kusumba (a sort of kidney bean), madhúka (Bassia Latifolia), and ingudi (Terminalia Catappa) will be one-fourth. p.134 one kusámra madhúka

P032 Five palas of kárpása (cotton) and of kshauma (flax) will yield one pala of threads. p.134 five one kárpása pala palas

P033 Rice prepared in such a way that five dróna of sáli yield ten ádhakas of rice will be fit to be the food of young elephants; eleven ádhakas from five drónas for elephants of bad temper (vyála); ten ádhakas from the same quantity for elephants trained for riding; nine ádhakas from the same quantity for elephants used in war; eight ádhakas from the same for infantry; eleven ádhakas from the same for chiefs of the army; six ádhakas from the same for queens and princes and five ádhakas from the same quantity for kings. p.134 eight eleven five nine six ten king prince queen dróna drónas sáli vyála ádhakas adhakas drona dronas

P034 One prastha of rice, pure and unsplit, one-fourth prastha of súpa, and clarified butter or oil equal to one-fourth part of (súpa) will suffice to form one meal of an Arya. p.134 one súpa prastha

P035 One-sixth prastha of súpa for a man; and half the above quantity of oil will form one meal for low castes (avara). p.134 half one súpa prastha

P036 The same rations less by one-fourth the above quantities will form one meal for a woman; and half the above rations for children. p.135 half one

P037 For dressing twenty palas of flesh, half a kutumba of oil, one pala of salt, one pala of sugar (kshára), two dharanas of pungent substances (katuka, spices), and half a prastha of curd (will be necessary). p.135 two dharanas half one twenty two kshára pala palas prastha

P038 For dressing greater quantities of flesh, the same ingredients can be proportionally increased. p.135

P039 For cooking sákas (dried fish and vegetables), the above substances are to be added one and a half times as much. p.135 half one sákas

P040 For dressing dried fish, the above ingredients are to be added twice as much. p.135 twice

P041 Measures of rations for elephants and horses will be described in connection with the "Duties of Their Respective Superintendents." p.135

P042 For bullocks, one drona of masha (Phraseolus Radiatus) or one drona of barley cooked with other things, as prescribed for horses, is the requisite quantity of food, besides the special and additional provision of one tula of oilcakes (ghánapinyaka) or ten ádhakas of bran (kanakuttana-kundaka). p.135 one ten ghánapinyaka ádhakas adhakas drona masha tula

P043 Twice the above quantity for buffaloes and camels. p.135 twice

P044 Half a drona for asses, red spotted deer and deer with white stripes. p.135 half whipping drona

P045 One ádhaka for an antelope and big red deer. p.136 one ádhaka adhaka

P046 Half an ádhaka or one ádhaka of grain together with bran for a goat, a ram and a boar. p.136 half one ádhaka adhaka

P047 One prastha of cooked rice for dogs. p.136 one prastha

P048 Half a prastha for a hamsa (goose), a krauncha (heron) and a peacock. p.136 half prastha

P049 From the above, the quantity of rations enough for one meal for other beasts, cattle, birds, and rogue elephants (vyála) may be inferred. p.136 one vyála

P050 Charcoal and chaff may be given over for iron smelting and lime-kiln (bhittilepya). p.136

P051 Bran and flour (kánika) may be given to slaves, labourers, and cooks. The surplus of the above may be given to those who prepare cooked rice, and rice-cakes. p.136 labourer slave kánika

P052 The weighing balance, weights, measures, mill-stone (rochani), pestle, mortar, wooden contrivances for pounding rice, etc., (kuttakayantra), contrivances for splitting seeds into pieces (rochakayantra), winnowing fans, sieves (chálani) grain-baskets (kandoli), boxes, and brooms are the necessary instruments. p.136 chálani

P053 Sweepers; preservers; those who weigh things (dharaka); those who measure grains, etc.; those who supervise the work of measuring grains (mápaka); those who supervise the supply of commodities to the store-house (dápaka); those who supply commodities (dáyaka); those who are employed to receive compensation for any real or supposed error in measuring grains, etc. (sálákáipratigráhaka); slaves; and labourers;—all these are called vishti. p.136 labourer slave compensation dápaka dáyaka mápaka sálákáipratigráhaka

P054 Grains are heaped up on the floor; jaggory (kshára) is bound round in grass-rope (múta); oils are kept in earthenware or wooden vessels; and salt is heaped up on the surface of the ground. p.137 kshára múta

P055 [Thus ends Chapter XV, “The Superintendent of Storehouse,” in Book II, “The Duties of Government Superintendents” of the Arthasástra of Kautilya. End of the thirty-sixth chapter from the beginning.] p.137
