Chapter II: Protection of Merchants

B04.C02 · Book IV: The Removal of Thorns · pp. 291–295

Protection of Merchants

P001 THE Superintendent of Commerce shall allow the sale or mortgage of any old commodities (purána bhándanám) only when the seller or mortgagor of such articles proves his ownership of the same. With a view to prevent deception, he shall also supervise weights and measures. p.291 superintendent of commerce bhándanám purána

P002 Difference of half a pala in such measures as are called parimání and drona is no offence. But difference of a pala in them shall be punished with a fine of 12 panas. p.292 12 panas fine 12 panas 12 half fine punishment parimání drona pala

P003 Fines for greater differences shall be proportionally increased. p.292 fine

P004 Difference of a karsha in the balance called tulá is no offence. Difference of two karshas shall be punished with a fine of 6 panas. Fines for greater differences shall be proportionally increased. p.292 6 panas a karsha fine 6 panas two karshas 6 two fine punishment tulá karsha tula

P005 Difference of half a karsha in the measure called ádhaka is no offence; but difference of a karsha shall be punished with a fine of 3 panas. p.292 3 panas a karsha fine 3 panas 3 half fine punishment ádhaka adhaka karsha

P006 For greater differences, fines shall be proportionally increased. p.292 fine

P007 Fines for differences in weight in other kinds of balances shall be inferred on the basis of the above rule. p.292 fine

P008 When a merchant purchases by a false balance a greater quantity of a commodity and sells under the same nominal weight a less quantity by the same or another false balance, he shall be punished with double the above fines. p.292 double merchant fine punishment

P009 Deception on the part of a seller to the extent of ⅛th part of the articles valued at a pana and sold by number shall be punished with a fine of 96 panas. p.292 96 panas a pana fine 96 panas 1 8th 96 fine punishment

P010 The sale or mortgage of articles such as timber, iron, brilliant stones, ropes, skins, earthenware, threads, fibrous garments, and woollen clothes as superior though they are really inferior shall be punished with a fine of 8 times the value of the articles thus sold. p.292 fine 8 8 fine punishment

P011 When a trader sells or mortgages inferior as superior commodities, articles of some other locality, as the produce of a particular locality, adulterated things, or deceitful mixtures, or when he dexterously substitutes other articles for those just sold (samutparivartimam), he shall not only be punished with a fine of 54 panas but also be compelled to make good the loss. p.293 54 panas fine 54 panas 54 merchant fine punishment

P012 By making the fine two panas for the loss of the value of a pana, and 200 panas for that of 100, fines can be determined for any of such false sales. p.293 200 panas a pana fine two panas two panas 100 200 two fine

P013 Those who conspire to lower the quality of the works of artisans, to hinder their income, or to obstruct their sale or purchase shall be fined thousand panas. p.293 fine thousand panas thousand panas thousand artisan fine

P014 Merchants who conspire either to prevent the sale of merchandise or to sell or purchase commodities at higher prices shall be fined 1,000 panas. p.293 1,000 panas fine 1,000 panas 000 1 merchant fine

P015 Middlemen who cause to a merchant or a purchaser the loss of ⅛th of a pana by substituting with tricks of hand false weights or measures or other kinds of inferior articles shall be punished with a fine of 200 panas. p.293 200 panas a pana fine 200 panas 1 200 8th merchant fine punishment

P016 Fines for, greater losses shall be proportionally increased commencing from 200 panas. p.293 200 panas 200 fine

P017 Adulteration of grains, oils, alkalis, salts, scents, and medicinal articles with similar articles of no quality shall be punished with a fine of 12 panas. p.293 12 panas fine 12 panas 12 fine punishment

P018 It is the duty of the trader to calculate the daily earnings of middlemen and to fix that amount on which they are authorised to live; for whatever income falls between sellers and purchasers (i.e., brokerage) is different from profit. p.294 merchant

P019 Hence authorised persons alone shall collect grains and other merchandise. Collection of such things without permission shall be confiscated by the Superintendent of Commerce. p.294 superintendent of commerce confiscation

P020 Hence shall merchants be favourably disposed towards the people in selling grains and other commodities. p.294 merchant

P021 The Superintendent of Commerce shall fix a profit of five per cent over and above the fixed price of local commodities, and ten per cent on foreign produce. Merchants who enchance the price or realise profit even to the extent of half a pana more than the above in the sale or purchase of commodities shall be punished with a fine of from five panas in case of realising 100 panas up to 200 panas. p.294 100 panas 200 panas a pana five panas 100 200 five half ten merchant superintendent of commerce fine punishment

P022 Fines for greater enhancement shall be proportionally increased. p.294 fine

P023 In case of failure to sell collected merchandise wholesale at a fixed rate, the rate shall be altered. p.294

P024 In case of obstruction to traffic, the Superintendent shall show necessary concessions. p.294

P025 Whenever there is an excessive supply of merchandise, the Superintendent shall centralise its sale and prohibit the sale of similar merchandise elsewhere before the centralised supply is disposed of. p.294

P026 Favourably disposed towards the people, shall merchants sell this centralised supply for daily wages. p.294 merchant

P027 The Superintendent shall, on consideration of the outlay, the quantity manufactured, the amount of toll, the interest on outlay, hire, and other kinds of accessory expenses, fix the price of such merchandise with due regard to its having been manufactured long ago or imported from a distant country (desakálántaritánám panyánám.) p.295 desakálántaritánám panyánám

P028 [Thus ends Chapter II, “Protection of merchants” in Book IV “The Removal of Thorns” of the Arthasástra of Kautilya. End of the seventy-ninth chapter from the beginning.] p.295

[Thus ends Chapter II, “Protection of merchants” in Book IV “The Removal of Thorns” of the Arthasástra of Kautilya. End of the seventy-ninth chapter from the beginning.]
